Viva-Voce Guide & Circuit Equations

1. Study of Rectifier & Capacitive Filter (Exp. 2 & 8)

The 230V, 50Hz AC supply steps down to $15\text{V}_{\text{rms}}$ AC. Four 1N4007 silicon diodes form a full-wave bridge rectifier (dropping $2 \times 0.7\text{V} = 1.4\text{V}$), smoothed by the $2200\mu\text{F}$ electrolytic capacitor:

V_peak = 15V × √2 ≈ 21.21V
V_dc(unregulated) = 21.21V - 1.4V ≈ 19.8V DC

2. Active Components & Voltage Regulation (Exp. 6)

An LM317 adjustable linear voltage regulator sets the float-charging rail at $13.8\text{V}$ using resistor divider $R_1 = 240\,\Omega$ and $R_2 = 2400\,\Omega$:

V_reg = 1.25V × (1 + R2 / R1) = 1.25V × (1 + 2400 / 240) = 13.75V ≈ 13.8V

3. KVL, Superposition & Zero-Delay Diode Switching (Exp. 3 & 4)

Two 1N5822 Schottky diodes ($V_f \approx 0.35\text{V}$) form a hardware OR-gate at the output node:

  • Mains ON: Regulator feeds $13.8\text{V} - 0.35\text{V} = 13.45\text{V}$ to the router. Since cathode of $D_2$ is at $13.45\text{V}$ and battery anode is at $12.0\text{V}$, diode $D_2$ is reverse-biased (blocking battery discharge) while resistor $R_{\text{chg}}$ trickle-charges the battery.
  • Mains OFF (Blackout): Regulator voltage drops to $0\text{V}$. Diode $D_2$ immediately becomes forward-biased, supplying $12.0\text{V} - 0.35\text{V} = 11.65\text{V}$ to the router in nanoseconds so the Wi-Fi never reboots.

4. Electrical Protection Equipment (Exp. 1 & 5)

The primary side is guarded by a single-pole Miniature Circuit Breaker (MCB) and a $0.5\text{A}$ glass cartridge fuse, while the DC output features a $2\text{A}$ fast-blow fuse to protect both the battery and router from short-circuits.
